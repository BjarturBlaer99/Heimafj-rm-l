# Financial product design research

Reviewed 16 September 2026. These are useful product references, not an objective ranking of design quality or a test of AI authorship.

## References and decisions

- [Mercury Transactions](https://mercury.com/blog/updated-transactions-page): the cash-flow summary and transaction table share filters. Its [public demo](https://demo.mercury.com/) also exposes clear navigation, compact controls, and a consistent row structure. Applied: transaction ledger first, one filter toolbar, restrained totals, secondary entry and import areas.
- [Copilot dashboard](https://help.copilot.money/en/articles/6045480-dashboard-tab-overview): prioritizes spending, reviewable transactions, upcoming payments and defined monthly net income. Applied: monthly income minus recorded expenses is explicitly labeled; unpaid bills are separate. No account-balance claims from cash-flow data.
- [Monarch tracking](https://www.monarch.com/features/tracking): distinguishes transactions, reports, recurring payments and net worth, with dashboard links into more detailed views. Applied: predictable navigation and clear page purposes across overview, transactions, bills, and savings.
- [Wise list items](https://wise.design/components/list-item): consistent row anatomy, meaningful short text, readable values, and status conveyed through more than color. Applied: aligned financial amounts, explicit payment status, quieter dividers, readable labels, and mobile stacking.
- [Copilot product presentation](https://www.copilot.money/): presents recognizable product UI and specific workflows. Applied: a separate public introduction with clearly labeled sample figures and a working link to the complete demo, alongside sign-up and login.

## Mín fjármál design direction

An Icelandic financial workspace with warm neutral backgrounds, white working surfaces, ink-colored type, and a restrained blue action color. Serif page headings distinguish the identity; financial rows and forms retain readable sans-serif text and tabular numbers. Navigation, fields, button sizes, section spacing, and feedback share the same rules across screens.

The design avoids invented testimonials, usage statistics, bank connections, security certifications, and decorative status scores. Existing finance actions, date-control fixes, success feedback, and reduced-motion behavior remain part of the product.

User preference after preview: keep information, filters, and editing forms open in the scrolling page instead of using expandable panels. Apply one consistent fade-in entrance to content sections as they enter the viewport. Keep keyboard focus and anchor targets immediately visible, and respect reduced-motion preferences.

This research informed design judgments; it does not prove a particular aesthetic is universally best.
